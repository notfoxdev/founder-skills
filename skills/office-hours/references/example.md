# Worked example: office-hours

> Illustrative and fictional (Ledgerline fixture). Calibrates depth and tone only; never reuse its content.

## Request

"Office hours. Am I fooling myself with this?"

## Context used

- `.foundora/company.md`: Ledgerline, close tracking for small accounting firms. Stage: MVP, 2 free pilots, 11 interviews, 62 waitlist signups. Biggest risk: firms won't pay on top of their practice software.
- Stage routing: users, not paying, so Q2, Q4 and Q5.

## Model answer (first turn)

You have two firms using it and nobody paying, so I'll skip the "is there a problem" questions and go where your risk is.

First one: what are those two firms doing today, outside Ledgerline, to make sure close happens on time? Not what they'd like to do. What actually happens on the 3rd of the month, who does it, and how long does it take?

## Model answer (after the founder replies "They use a shared spreadsheet and the managing partner checks it")

That's a real workaround, which is good: someone already spends time on this. But a spreadsheet the partner checks is cheap, so you're competing with ten minutes a week, not with a missed deadline. I'd believe there's money here if either firm missed a close in the last quarter. Did they?

## Model answer (closing note, abridged)

# Office hours: Ledgerline (2026-10-23)

**Where it stands:** Two firms use it weekly, the status quo is a spreadsheet the partner checks, and nobody has been asked to pay.
**Mode:** Startup

## The assignment (by 2026-10-30)
Ask both pilot firms to convert at $150 a month starting November 1. If neither agrees, the problem is real but not expensive enough, and we look at the firms that missed a close.

## What I noticed
You said "the partner checks it" twice. The person who checks is your buyer, not the bookkeeper you've been interviewing.
