# Call kit

Load this when building a prep sheet, choosing discovery questions, planning a demo, writing up notes, or drafting a follow-up. Everything here is a starting point. Swap in the prospect's words wherever you have them.

## Prep sheet: how to fill it in

Fifteen to twenty minutes, one page. If it takes longer, you're researching instead of prepping.

1. **Call goal first.** "<who> agrees to <next step> by <date>." If you can't write it, you don't know why you're having the call.
2. **What we know.** Every fact gets a source: founder, their email, their website (with the page), a past call (with the date). Anything without a source is `[UNVERIFIED]` and you don't mention it on the call.
3. **Pain hypothesis.** One or two lines, in their words if a past call or email gave you any. If not, mark it `[ASSUMPTION]` and make confirming it the first thing to learn.
4. **Three things to learn.** Usually: the last time the problem bit, who pays, and what would make them act this month.
5. **The ask, word for word.** Writing it down makes it far more likely you'll say it.
6. **Walk-away line.** The answer that means this isn't a customer right now. Deciding it before the call stops you chasing a polite maybe.

## Discovery question bank

Pick 6–8. Mark the 3–4 you must ask if time runs short with `[MUST]`. Every question asks about something that already happened.

**The problem**
- "Tell me about the last time <the problem> happened. What was going on?"
- "Who noticed first? What did they do?"
- "How often does that happen? When was the time before?"

**The workaround**
- "What do you do about it today? Can you show me?"
- "Who keeps that going? How long does it take them?"
- "What have you tried that didn't stick? Why did it stop?"

**The cost**
- "What did that one cost you, in hours or money or anything else?"
- "What happens if it goes wrong at the worst time?"
- Let them name the number. If they can't, that's information too.

**Who's involved**
- "Who else deals with this?"
- "If you wanted to change how it's done, who would need to agree?"
- "Who pays for the tools you use for this today?"

**How they buy**
- "What's the last tool you added? Who found it, and who said yes?"
- "How did you pay for it: card, invoice, something else?"
- "How long from first look to using it?"

**Reason to act now**
- "Where does this sit against everything else this month?"
- "Is anything coming up that makes it worse, or more urgent?"

**Don't ask:** "Would you use something that...?", "Would you pay for...?", "Is this a problem for you?" They invite polite yeses. Ask what happened instead.

## Demo plan

Build it from their words, not from your product's menu.

| Their words (from discovery) | What you show | Check-in question |
|---|---|---|
| <the pain, quoted> | <the one screen or moment that answers it> | "How does that compare to last month?" |

- 1–3 rows. If you have more, you're touring features.
- Load their data, or data shaped like theirs, before the call. Never a real customer's data from another account.
- Test the demo path once on the day. If something breaks live, say so, show the next row, and send a short recording afterwards.
- End the demo when they've seen their problem handled. Then ask, and be quiet.

## Follow-up templates

Rules for every one of them: under 120 words, plain text, sent the same day, in the founder's voice. Their pain in their words. One next step with a date. Read it as their sharpest customer would before sending.

**After a first call**
> Hi <name>, thanks for walking me through <their workaround> today. What stuck with me was <their words about the pain>. As agreed, <next step> on <day, date>; the invite's in your calendar. I said I'd find out <question> and I'll send it by <day>.

**After a demo**
> Hi <name>, thanks for the time today. You said <their reaction, quoted>. The open question was <objection or gap>; <answer, or "I'm checking and will send it by <day>">. Next is <step> on <date>. If anything changes before then, just reply here.

**Sending pilot terms**
> Hi <name>, here are the terms we talked about, so they're in one place:
> - <price> a month from <start date>
> - We'll measure <what>; it's working if <pass line> by <end date>
> - If it isn't, you stop and owe nothing more
> If that matches what you expected, a reply with "yes" is enough and I'll send the payment link.

**A summary the user can forward to the buyer**
> <buyer>, I talked to <founder> at <company> about <the problem, in the user's words>. They showed me <the one thing>. Can we give them 20 minutes this week? <founder> has <day> or <day> free.

**After silence (once, about a week after a missed step)**
> Hi <name>, we'd planned <step> for <date> and I haven't heard back, which is fine. Is this still worth doing, or should I close it out on my side for now? Either answer helps.

**After a no**
> Thanks for being straight with me, <name>. If it's useful to know, what tipped it? Either way, I'd like to check back in <month>, if that's alright.
