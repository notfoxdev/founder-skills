# Formats

Read this after choosing a format in step 1. Timings are defaults; scale proportionally if the runtime changes.

## Launch video (60–90s)

Purpose: make the launch feel real, show the product doing its one thing, and give people something to share.

| Beat | 60s | 90s | Content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 0:00–0:02 | 0:00–0:02 | Result, pattern interrupt, or the painful moment |
| Problem | 0:02–0:10 | 0:02–0:14 | The customer's current workaround, shown on screen or acted in one shot |
| Turn | 0:10–0:12 | 0:14–0:17 | One line or one hard cut into the product |
| Product moment | 0:12–0:42 | 0:17–1:05 | 2–3 key actions, real UI, zoomed to what matters |
| Proof | 0:42–0:52 | 1:05–1:18 | Real customer, real number, or honest stage line ("In beta with 9 bakeries") |
| CTA | 0:52–1:00 | 1:18–1:30 | Action + URL on screen; end card held 2–3s |

Notes:
- Founder on camera works for the hook, turn, and CTA; the product moment is screen.
- Make a 16:9 master, then cut a 1:1 or 4:5 for LinkedIn/X feeds and a 9:16 for short-form.
- Product Hunt gallery video: at the time of writing the listing takes a YouTube link; verify current requirements in the submission form. Keep it under about 2 minutes and front-load the product (heuristic).
- A "demo" of 90 seconds or less uses these beats with the product moment stretched to 50–60% of runtime.

## Product demo (over 90s, typically 2–3 min)

Purpose: help a qualified viewer understand how it works for their case. Viewers here chose to watch; clarity beats pace.

| Beat | Time (3 min) | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 0:00–0:05 | The outcome they will see by the end ("By the end you will see a full month of invoices reconciled") |
| Context | 0:05–0:20 | Who this is for and the situation (one sentence each) |
| Setup | 0:20–0:45 | The first step, shown as small as it really is (connect, import, invite) |
| Core workflow | 0:45–2:15 | The main job end to end, in 3–4 chapters, each with a title card or spoken chapter line |
| Edge or objection | 2:15–2:35 | One thing buyers worry about (permissions, errors, export) handled on screen |
| Proof + CTA | 2:35–3:00 | Stage or customer proof, then one next step (trial, book a call) |

Notes:
- Add YouTube chapters (timestamps in description, first at 0:00, at least 3 chapters, each 10s+; verify current YouTube rules).
- Speak to one viewer. "You" not "users".
- Cut dead time: loading, typing long strings, navigation. Use jump cuts or speed ramps, not waiting.
- For sales follow-up, record a version that names the prospect's use case only if the founder sends it individually.

## Short-form (15–45s, vertical)

Purpose: earn attention from people who did not ask to see you. Retention through the first 3 seconds matters more than anything after.

| Beat | 15s | 30s | 45s | Content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 0–2s | 0–2s | 0–2s | Visual + spoken + text aligned |
| Setup | 2–4s | 2–7s | 2–10s | The problem or the promise |
| Payoff | 4–12s | 7–25s | 10–38s | The product doing the thing, fast |
| CTA / loop | 12–15s | 25–30s | 38–45s | Soft CTA ("link in bio", "comment X") or a loop back to the hook |

Notes:
- 1080x1920, 30fps (60 for UI motion), native platform captions or burned-in captions.
- Cut every 1.5–3s. A static shot longer than 4s needs motion in it (zoom, cursor, text).
- Text and faces inside safe zones (see SKILL.md step 7; approximate, verify against the current platform overlay).
- Design the last frame to loop into the first where possible; replays count.
- One idea per video. Series beat one-offs: a recurring format ("One spreadsheet we killed this week") is easier to keep shipping.
- Platform behaviour and length limits change often. Check current limits before publishing; stay in 15–45s regardless.

Platform tendencies (verify current norms):
- **TikTok:** native feel, founder talking to camera, trends and sounds; overly polished ads underperform organically.
- **Instagram Reels:** slightly more polished; the cover frame matters on the profile grid.
- **YouTube Shorts:** strong search and subscriber spillover; clear titles help.
- **X:** autoplays muted in the feed; the first frame and a strong text overlay carry it; post the video natively, not as a link.
- **LinkedIn:** 1:1 or 4:5 often fills more of the feed than 9:16; captions essential; B2B buyers respond to the founder explaining a problem plainly.

## Hero loop (6–12s, silent)

- No audio, no captions needed if the loop shows UI that reads on its own; add a single text card only if necessary.
- Show one action from start to finish (drag a file in, result appears).
- Export MP4 (H.264) and WebM, target under about 3MB, plus an AVIF/JPG poster frame.
- Muted, `playsinline`, loop, with a pause control if it runs longer than 5 seconds. Show the poster under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Screen recording

- Record at 1920x1080 or 2560x1440 with browser zoom at 125–150% so UI text is legible on phones. Export at 1080p minimum.
- Clean browser profile: no bookmarks bar, no extensions, no personal tabs, notifications off (OS and app).
- Seed a demo account with plausible sample data. Label invented data "Sample data" in a corner if a viewer could mistake it for a real customer. No real customer PII.
- Hide the cursor when idle; enlarge or highlight it when it matters. Tools like Screen Studio or Descript add automatic zooms; set zoom to 1.5–2x on the element being used.
- Rehearse each flow 2–3 times, then record each chapter as a separate take.
- Type fast strings with a text expander or paste, then speed up in the edit.

## Founder on camera

- Light: face a window or a soft light at about 45 degrees, slightly above eye level. Avoid overhead light and a bright window behind.
- Audio matters more than video. Use a lavalier or USB mic 15–30cm from the mouth; record in a room with soft furnishings. Turn off fans and air conditioning.
- Camera at eye level, framed chest-up, eyes about one third from the top. Phone rear camera beats most webcams.
- Read from a teleprompter app or record in short chunks. Look at the lens, not the screen.
- Wear solid colours; avoid fine stripes (moire) and logos you do not own.

## B-roll

- Use it to show the problem context: the cluttered spreadsheet, the clipboard on the warehouse floor, the whiteboard of shifts. Real environments beat stock.
- Stock footage only for neutral context, never as proof, never as "customers".
- Every B-roll shot must relate to the line it sits under. Decorative B-roll is a tell.
- Shoot 3–5 seconds per shot, steady, with a slow push or pan for motion.

## Edit and sound

- Pace: tight on hook and problem, a beat slower on the key product moment so viewers can read the UI.
- Music: licensed, instrumental, sitting about 15–20dB under the voice. Duck under speech.
- Loudness: about -14 LUFS integrated is a common target for social and YouTube; peaks below -1 dBTP. Platforms normalise differently and change it; verify current guidance.
- Captions: sentence case, 1–2 lines, max about 32–42 characters per line, synced to speech. Check auto-captions by hand; product names are often wrong.
- End card: logo, URL, the CTA words, held 2–3 seconds.

## Cutdown plan

From one launch video master, typical derived versions:

| Version | Source range | Aspect | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hero loop | Product moment, 6–12s | 16:10 | Landing page hero |
| 30s social | Hook + product moment + CTA | 9:16 and 1:1 | Short-form, X, LinkedIn |
| 15s teaser | Hook + one payoff | 9:16 | Pre-launch teaser |
| GIF | 3–6s of the key action | 16:10 | Product Hunt gallery, README, email |
