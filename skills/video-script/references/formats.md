# Formats

Read this after choosing a format in step 1. Timings are defaults; scale proportionally if the runtime changes.

## Launch video (30s, 60s, 90s)

Purpose: make the launch feel real, show the product doing its one thing, and give people something to share. Concepts and second-by-second beats are in `launch-playbook.md`.

- Founder on camera works for the hook, turn, and CTA; the product moment is screen or motion.
- Make a 16:9 master, then a 1:1 or 4:5 for LinkedIn and X feeds and a 9:16 for short-form (see `platforms.md`).
- A "demo" of 90 seconds or less uses launch beats with the product moment stretched to 50 to 60% of runtime.

## Product demo (over 90s, typically 2 to 3 min)

Purpose: help a qualified viewer understand how it works for their case. Viewers here chose to watch; clarity beats pace.

| Beat | Time (3 min) | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 0:00 to 0:05 | The outcome they will see by the end ("By the end you will see a full month of invoices reconciled") |
| Context | 0:05 to 0:20 | Who this is for and the situation (one sentence each) |
| Setup | 0:20 to 0:45 | The first step, shown as small as it really is (connect, import, invite) |
| Core workflow | 0:45 to 2:15 | The main job end to end, in 3 to 4 chapters, each with a title card or spoken chapter line |
| Edge or objection | 2:15 to 2:35 | One thing buyers worry about (permissions, errors, export) handled on screen |
| Proof + CTA | 2:35 to 3:00 | Stage or customer proof, then one next step (trial, book a call) |

Notes:
- Add YouTube chapters (rules in `platforms.md`, verify current).
- Speak to one viewer. "You", not "users".
- Cut dead time with jump cuts or speed ramps, not waiting (`demo-craft.md`).
- For sales follow-up, record a version that names the prospect's use case only if the founder sends it individually.

## Short-form (15 to 45s, vertical)

Purpose: earn attention from people who did not ask to see you. Retention through the first 3 seconds matters more than anything after.

| Beat | 15s | 30s | 45s | Content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 0 to 2s | 0 to 2s | 0 to 2s | Visual + spoken + text aligned |
| Setup | 2 to 4s | 2 to 7s | 2 to 10s | The problem or the promise |
| Payoff | 4 to 12s | 7 to 25s | 10 to 38s | The product doing the thing, fast |
| CTA / loop | 12 to 15s | 25 to 30s | 38 to 45s | Soft CTA ("link in bio", "comment X") or a loop back to the hook |

Notes:
- 1080x1920, 30fps (60 for fast UI motion), burned-in captions inside the safe zone (`demo-craft.md`).
- Cut every 1.5 to 3s. A static shot longer than 4s needs motion in it (zoom, cursor, text).
- Design the last frame to loop into the first where possible; replays count.
- One idea per video. A recurring series ("One spreadsheet we killed this week") is easier to keep shipping than one-offs.

Platform tendencies (heuristics, verify current norms):
- **TikTok:** native feel, founder talking to camera; polished ads underperform organically.
- **Instagram Reels:** slightly more polished; the cover frame matters on the profile grid.
- **YouTube Shorts:** search and subscriber spillover; clear titles help.
- **X:** autoplays muted; the first frame and a text overlay carry it.
- **LinkedIn:** 1:1 or 4:5 fills more of the feed than 9:16; B2B buyers respond to the founder explaining a problem plainly.

## Hero loop (6 to 10s, silent)

- One action from start to finish (drag a file in, result appears); last frame matches the first.
- No audio track. A single text card only if the UI cannot read on its own.
- File budget, codecs, and markup in `platforms.md`.

## Founder on camera

- Light: face a window or a soft light at about 45 degrees, slightly above eye level. Avoid overhead light and a bright window behind.
- Audio matters more than video. Use a lavalier or USB mic 15 to 30cm from the mouth; record in a room with soft furnishings.
- Camera at eye level, framed chest-up, eyes about one third from the top. A phone rear camera beats most webcams.
- Read from a teleprompter app or record in short chunks. Look at the lens, not the screen.
- Wear solid colours; avoid fine stripes (moire) and logos you do not own.

## B-roll

- Use it to show the problem context: the cluttered spreadsheet, the clipboard on the warehouse floor, the whiteboard of shifts. Real environments beat stock.
- Stock footage only for neutral context, never as proof, never as "customers".
- Every B-roll shot relates to the line it sits under. Decorative B-roll is a tell.
- Shoot 3 to 5 seconds per shot, steady, with a slow push or pan for motion.

## Edit

- Pace: tight on hook and problem, a beat slower on the key product moment so viewers can read the UI.
- Captions: sentence case, 1 to 2 lines, synced to speech, line and rate limits per SKILL.md step 9. Check auto-captions by hand; product names are often wrong.
- End card: wordmark, URL, the CTA words, held 2 to 3 seconds.
- Music, SFX, and loudness: `audio.md`.
