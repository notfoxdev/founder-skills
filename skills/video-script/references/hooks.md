# Hooks

Read this when writing the three hook options in step 5. A hook is the first 1 to 2 seconds: one image, one spoken line, one text overlay, all saying the same thing.

## Find the product's real moment first

Before writing any hook, name the one frame where the value becomes visible: the tick appearing, the report filling in, the error caught, the file landing. Write it down as "the moment: <frame>". Every hook either shows that moment (outcome first) or the exact pain it removes (problem first). A hook that could open any other product's video fails the swap test; rewrite it.

## First 1 to 2 seconds rules

- Frame 1 is already interesting. Mid-scroll, mid-click, mid-sentence. No fade from black, no build-up, no establishing shot.
- Show the outcome first when the output is visual: the finished state, then "here's how". Show the pain first when the outcome only makes sense against the old way.
- Picture first. On muted autoplay the visual and text overlay carry the hook alone; the spoken line confirms it.
- Spoken line 5 words or fewer. Text overlay 2 to 7 words, restating, not competing.
- Specific beats clever: a real object, a real job, the customer's own words, a founder-provided number.
- Motion in the first 0.5s: a cursor moving, a cell changing, a hand entering. A static first frame reads as a thumbnail and gets scrolled.
- The hook is paid off on screen, and the script states the payoff timestamp. A promise the video does not show burns trust and retention.
- Only true claims. Any number in a hook is founder-provided and checkable.

## Patterns

Fill the brackets with real specifics from `company.md`.

### 1. Outcome first
- Visual: the finished artifact or end state filling the frame, then a rewind.
- Spoken: "This is [the result] for [real scope]."
- Works when: the output is visual and obviously valuable. Default for watch-it-work.
- Risk: the result must be real and reproducible on camera, or labelled as a concept.

### 2. The painful moment
- Visual: the messy spreadsheet mid-scroll, the 40-tab browser, the paper rota.
- Spoken: "[Exact thing they say when it goes wrong]."
- Works when: the pain is specific and recognisable. Default for problem-first.
- Risk: reads as a skit if acted. Show the real artifact, not someone sighing.

### 3. Call out the viewer
- Visual: the audience's own tool on screen, or founder to camera.
- Spoken: "If you [do X] for [narrow audience], watch this."
- Works when: the audience is narrow; strong on LinkedIn and TikTok.
- Risk: too broad ("If you run a business") calls out nobody.

### 4. Watch me do X in Y
- Visual: a visible timer, product open, first action already moving.
- Spoken: "Watch me [do the job] in [real seconds]."
- Works when: speed is the value and you measured it on camera.
- Risk: the time must be true, unedited, or with cuts disclosed.

### 5. Side by side
- Visual: split frame, same task, both running.
- Spoken: "Same [task]. Two ways."
- Works when: the alternative is familiar and the difference shows in one frame.
- Risk: misrepresenting a named competitor. Compare against the workaround.

### 6. The contrarian line
- Visual: founder to camera, direct.
- Spoken: "[Common practice] is costing you [real consequence]."
- Works when: the founder has earned the opinion and the video backs it up.
- Risk: a hot take without substance.

### 7. The question they actually ask
- Visual: the question as text over the problem footage.
- Spoken: "How do you [specific job] without [workaround]?"
- Works when: mined from interview or search language; good for Shorts and YouTube.
- Risk: generic questions ("Want to save time?") get skipped.

### 8. Pattern interrupt
- Visual: an unexpected but relevant action. A stack of paper invoices dropped on a desk, a spreadsheet cell ticked with a red pen, a whiteboard of client names wiped clean, a hard zoom from the full sheet to one cell.
- Spoken: a short line connecting it to the product.
- Works when: the object or move belongs to the customer's world and the product story.
- Risk: a gimmick unrelated to the product. Random props, glitch effects, and jump scares are not interrupts, they are noise.

### 9. Founder confession
- Visual: founder to camera, or a real archival photo.
- Spoken: "I [did this job] for [real years]. The worst part was [moment]."
- Works when: the founder's history is the proof; launch videos and LinkedIn.
- Risk: preamble. The product is on screen by 10 to 13s.

### 10. The number
- Visual: the number large on screen, then the context.
- Spoken: "[Real number] [unit] every [period]."
- Works when: the number comes from the founder's data or a cited public source.
- Risk: invented or unsourced numbers. If unsure, use another pattern.

## Banned openers and the fix

| Opener | Why it fails | Replace with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logo sting or animated title card first | Spends the 2 seconds on you, not the viewer | The product's real moment; logo on the end card only |
| "Introducing [product]" | Announces instead of showing | Outcome first |
| "Meet [product]" | Same, and the viewer has not asked to meet anything | The painful moment, then the product as the turn |
| "In today's world..." / "In today's fast-paced..." | Generic, could open any video | The specific object from the customer's day |
| "Hi, I'm [name], founder of [company]" | Credentials before relevance | Founder confession that starts on the moment; name in the lower third |
| "Are you tired of...?" / "What if I told you..." | Infomercial pattern, instantly skipped | The question they actually ask, in their words |
| "You won't believe..." | Clickbait, and B2B buyers discount it | One real number or result |
| Slow fade from black, ambient intro, drone shot | Nothing happens in the first second | Start mid-action |
| Stock montage of smiling people | Signals no real product or customer | Real UI, real workspace, real founder |
| "3 reasons why..." | Only works if the content is a list | A single claim, shown |
| Any hook that needs sound | Muted autoplay kills it | Picture plus text carry it alone |

## Hook option template

```markdown
1. Pattern: <name>
   The moment: <the frame where value becomes visible>
   Visual (0:00 to 0:02): <exact first frame and its motion in the first 0.5s>
   First line: "<5 words or fewer>"
   Text overlay: "<2 to 7 words>"
   Payoff at: <timestamp where the video delivers the hook>
   Why for this viewer/platform: <one sentence>
```
