# Hooks

Read this when writing the three hook options in step 3. A hook is the first 2 seconds: one image, one spoken line, one text overlay, all saying the same thing.

## Hook rules

- Start mid-action. The first frame is already interesting; no fade-in, no logo, no "Hey guys".
- Picture first. On muted autoplay, the visual and text overlay carry the hook alone.
- The text overlay restates the spoken line in 2–7 words; it does not compete with it.
- The hook must be paid off in the video. A hook that promises something the video does not show burns trust and retention.
- Specific beats clever. A real number, a real object, a real job.
- Only true claims. Any number in a hook is founder-provided and checkable.

## Patterns

Each pattern: structure, when it works, and the risk. Fill the brackets with real specifics from `company.md`.

### 1. Result first
Show the finished outcome, then rewind to how.
- Spoken: "This [report / schedule / invoice batch] took [real time]."
- Visual: the finished artifact filling the frame.
- Works when: the output is visual and obviously valuable.
- Risk: the result must be real and reproducible on camera.

### 2. The painful moment
Open inside the problem the viewer lives with.
- Spoken: "[Exact thing they say when it goes wrong]."
- Visual: the messy spreadsheet, the 47 unread messages, the paper rota.
- Works when: the pain is specific and recognisable to the target customer.
- Risk: it reads as a skit if overacted. Keep it plain.

### 3. Call out the viewer
Name exactly who this is for.
- Spoken: "If you [run / manage / do X] for [narrow audience], watch this."
- Visual: founder to camera, or the audience's tool on screen.
- Works when: the audience is narrow and identifiable; strong on LinkedIn and TikTok.
- Risk: too broad ("If you run a business") and it calls out nobody.

### 4. Watch me do X in Y
A real timed demonstration.
- Spoken: "Watch me [do the job] in [real seconds]."
- Visual: a visible timer or clock, product open.
- Works when: speed is the value and you have measured it.
- Risk: the time must be true on camera, unedited or with cuts disclosed.

### 5. Side by side
Old way vs new way in a split frame.
- Spoken: "Left is how [audience] does this today. Right is [product]."
- Visual: split screen, same task, both running.
- Works when: the alternative is familiar and the difference is visible.
- Risk: misrepresenting a named competitor. Compare against the workaround, or keep competitor comparisons factual and current.

### 6. The contrarian line
A defensible opinion the audience has felt but not said.
- Spoken: "[Common practice in their field] is costing you [real consequence]."
- Visual: founder to camera, direct.
- Works when: the founder has earned the opinion and the video backs it up.
- Risk: hot takes without substance; must be true and supported in the video.

### 7. The question they actually ask
The exact question the audience types into search or asks peers.
- Spoken: "How do you [specific job] without [common workaround]?"
- Visual: the question as text over the problem footage.
- Works when: mined from real customer language; good for Shorts and search-driven platforms.
- Risk: generic questions ("Want to save time?") are skipped.

### 8. Pattern interrupt
An unexpected visual tied to the subject.
- Visual: a physical object from the customer's world (a stack of paper invoices dropped on a desk, a whiteboard wiped clean).
- Spoken: a short line that connects it to the product.
- Works when: the object is part of the story, not random.
- Risk: gimmick if the object has nothing to do with the product.

### 9. Founder confession
A short, true story about why this exists.
- Spoken: "I [did this job] for [real years]. The worst part was [specific moment]."
- Visual: founder to camera, or archival photo if real.
- Works when: the founder's history is the proof; strong for launch videos and LinkedIn.
- Risk: long preamble. The story must reach the product within 10 seconds.

### 10. The number
One real, surprising figure.
- Spoken: "[Real number] [unit] of [thing] every [period]."
- Visual: the number large on screen, then the context.
- Works when: the number comes from the founder's data or a cited public source.
- Risk: invented or unsourced numbers. If unsure, use another pattern.

## Hooks to avoid

- Logo animation or title card.
- "Hi, I'm [name], founder of [company], and today..."
- "Are you tired of...?"
- "What if I told you..."
- "In today's world..."
- "You won't believe..."
- Countdown or "3 reasons why..." unless the content is genuinely a list.
- Any hook that requires sound to make sense on a silent feed.

## Hook option template

```markdown
1. Pattern: <name>
   Visual (0:00–0:02): <exact first frame and motion>
   First line: "<spoken words>"
   Text overlay: "<2–7 words>"
   Payoff at: <timestamp where the video delivers the hook>
   Why for this viewer/platform: <one sentence>
```
