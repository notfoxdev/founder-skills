# Interview kit reference

Patterns for screeners, outreach, the 30-minute script, and notes. Adapt wording to the segment and the research question; never send these verbatim without filling the placeholders.

## Screener patterns

Select on recent behaviour. A screener that asks "Do you struggle with X?" recruits people who like to agree.

| Purpose | Pattern | Qualify / disqualify |
|---|---|---|
| Role fit | "Which best describes your role?" (list 5–7 options, including decoys) | Qualify on target roles only |
| Recency | "When did you last <do the job>?" (this week / this month / 1–6 months / longer / never) | Qualify on this month or sooner |
| Workaround | "What did you use the last time?" (open text) | Disqualify blank or vague answers |
| Spend | "Roughly how much time or money goes into this each month?" (bands) | Use to segment, not to qualify, unless WTP is the question |
| Buying role | "Who decides on tools for this?" (me / me with approval / someone else) | Recruit a mix if testing buyer vs. user |
| Professional respondent check | "Have you taken part in research in the last 3 months?" | Disqualify yes for paid panels |
| Conflict | "Do you work for or with <list of competitors / founder's company>?" | Disqualify yes |

Rule: if fewer than 1 in 3 screener respondents qualify, the segment definition or the recruiting channel is wrong. Fix that before lowering the bar.

## Recruiting channels (ordered by typical quality for early-stage)

1. Warm introductions from people in the segment (ask each interviewee for two names).
2. Communities where the segment discusses the job (Slack and Discord groups, subreddits, professional forums, associations). Read the rules; ask moderators first.
3. Direct outreach to named people on LinkedIn or by email, targeted by role and recent activity.
4. The founder's existing users, waitlist, or support inbox.
5. Paid panels: fast, but screen hard and expect professional respondents.

## Outreach variants

Each under 90 words. Structure: why them, what you're learning (not selling), the ask with a time box, an easy out.

**Cold**
> Hi <name>, I'm researching how <role>s handle <job>. I saw <specific reason you picked them>. I'm not selling anything; I'm trying to understand how people do this today. Would you be open to a 20-minute call next week? Happy to share what I learn across the conversations. If it's not a fit, no reply needed.

**Warm introduction (for the introducer to forward)**
> <Introducer> mentioned you handle <job> at <company type>. I'm talking to a few people about how they do it today, especially <the specific part>. Would 20 minutes in the next two weeks work? I'll keep it to time.

**Community post**
> Mods OK'd this. I'm researching how <role>s handle <job>, especially <specific part>. Looking for 6–8 people to talk for 20–25 minutes about how you do it now. No pitch, no product to show. Comment or DM me and I'll send times.

Incentives: for B2B, offer a summary of findings; for consumers, a modest gift card is normal. An incentive large enough to attract people for the money skews the sample.

## 30-minute script structure

| Time | Section | Purpose | Example prompts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Open | Consent, set expectations | "I'm learning, not selling. There are no right answers. OK if I take notes / record?" |
| 3–8 | Context | Their role and where the job fits | "Walk me through a typical week. Where does <job> show up?" |
| 8–18 | Last time [MUST] | One specific recent instance, in detail | "Tell me about the last time you <did the job>. What triggered it? What did you do first? Then what?" |
| 18–23 | Workarounds and cost [MUST] | What they use and what it costs | "What have you tried? What do you pay for it, in money or hours? What's annoying about it?" |
| 23–26 | Priority and buying | Where this ranks; who decides | "Where does this rank among what you're dealing with this quarter? If you changed how you do it, who else would be involved?" |
| 26–30 | Close [MUST] | Commitment and referral | "Who else should I talk to? Could I follow up in two weeks with what I'm seeing?" (Pitch or demo only here, only if it serves the research question) |

Probes to use throughout:
- "Can you show me?" (screen share of the actual tool or sheet beats a description)
- "What happened next?"
- "Why was that hard?"
- "What did that cost you?"
- "You said 'usually'. When was the last time?"
- "What would you have done if that hadn't worked?"
- Silence for three seconds after an answer.

Questions never to ask, with replacements:
- "Would you use this?" -> "How do you handle it today?"
- "How much would you pay?" -> "What do you spend on it now?"
- "Is this a problem for you?" -> "What are the hardest parts of <job>?"

## Notes template

```markdown
# <ID> — <role / segment> — <YYYY-MM-DD> — <interviewer>
Recording: yes/no (consent given)  Notes type: verbatim / paraphrased
## Context
## Last time (story, in order)
## Workarounds and spend
## Pains (with cost if stated)
## Verbatim quotes (in quotation marks, word for word)
## Opinions / hypotheticals / feature requests (kept separate)
## Commitments asked / given
## Surprises
## Follow-up next time
```

## Within 10 minutes of each call

Write three lines at the top of the notes: the most surprising thing, the strongest piece of evidence, and what you'd change in the script. Update the script before the next call if a question consistently produces nothing.
