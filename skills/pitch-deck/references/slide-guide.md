# Slide guide

Per-slide guidance for pre-seed and seed decks. Each slide: job, what good looks like, common mistakes, visual. Numbers in examples are illustrative structure only; never reuse them.

## Stage-appropriate evidence

Investors judge evidence against stage. Do not apologise for stage; show the strongest evidence that stage allows.

| Stage | Strongest credible evidence | Weak evidence to avoid leading with |
|---|---|---|
| Idea | Founder-market fit, number of discovery interviews and the pattern found, waitlist with a stated source | "Everyone we talked to loved it" |
| Validation | Pre-orders, paid pilots, LOIs with named terms, smoke-test conversion, repeated pain quotes | Survey "would you use" percentages |
| MVP | Active usage, retention by cohort, design partners using it weekly, first paid conversions | Total sign-ups without activity |
| Early traction | Revenue (MRR/GMV), month-over-month growth, retention, sales cycle, payback indications | Cumulative charts that hide flat months |
| Growth | Net revenue retention, CAC payback, channel efficiency, cohort curves | Vanity totals (downloads, page views) |

If the evidence is thin, say what has been learned and what the raise will prove. A clear "here is what we will know in 6 months" beats padded metrics.

## 1. Title / one-liner
- **Job:** tell the reader what the company does in under 10 seconds.
- **Good:** company name, one plain-language line ("Payroll for restaurant chains with hourly staff"), founder name, contact, date. Optional second line with the strongest evidence, so the slide reads as the two-sentence description.
- **Mistakes:** taglines instead of descriptions ("Reimagining work"); category jargon ("AI-native orchestration layer"); wordplay a partner has to decode.
- **Visual:** logo, one line, nothing else. Optional product screenshot behind if it helps comprehension.

## 2. Problem
- **Job:** make a specific person's pain concrete and costly.
- **Good:** who has it (role, company type), what it costs (time, money, risk) with source, how often it happens, what they do today.
- **Mistakes:** macro problems ("healthcare is broken"); listing three problems; problems the customer does not feel.
- **Visual:** a short workflow showing the painful step, or one customer quote with role (founder-provided, with permission).

## 3. Solution / product
- **Job:** show how the product removes the pain from slide 2.
- **Good:** the before/after for the same person; 1–3 screenshots of the real product; the key mechanism in one sentence.
- **Mistakes:** feature lists; mockups presented as live product without saying so; architecture diagrams at pre-seed.
- **Visual:** annotated screenshot or 3-frame flow. Label mockups as mockups.

## 4. Why now
- **Job:** explain why this works now and did not five years ago.
- **Good:** one specific shift (new API or model capability, regulation date, cost drop, behaviour change, platform opening) with a date or source.
- **Mistakes:** "AI is big"; "the market is growing" (that is not a why-now).
- **Visual:** a timeline with the shift marked, or a before/after cost line.

## 5. Market
- **Job:** show the opportunity is large enough for a venture outcome, with credible math.
- **Good:** bottom-up: buyers × price × frequency. SOM tied to the go-to-market. See `market-sizing.md`.
- **Mistakes:** top-down only; three nested circles with unsourced billions; sizing a market the product does not serve yet.
- **Visual:** the multiplication written out, then the three numbers. Circles are fine only if the math is on the slide.

## 6. Traction / validation
- **Job:** prove that people want this, at the level the stage allows.
- **Good:** one headline metric with period and definition; growth rate; one retention or engagement signal; named logos only with permission.
- **Mistakes:** cumulative charts; mixing pilots with paying customers; unlabelled y-axis; "users" without a definition.
- **Visual:** a monthly bar chart (not cumulative), last 6–12 months, latest bar highlighted.

## 7. Business model
- **Job:** show how money is made and that unit economics can work.
- **Good:** pricing (actual or tested), who pays, contract shape, early ACV/AOV, gross margin expectation marked `[ASSUMPTION]` if not measured.
- **Mistakes:** listing five revenue streams; LTV:CAC at pre-seed with no data.
- **Visual:** a simple pricing table or a single unit-economics line.

## 8. Go-to-market
- **Job:** show how the first 100 (B2B) or 10,000 (B2C) customers arrive.
- **Good:** the one or two channels in use, what each has produced so far, the next channel to test, sales motion (self-serve, inside sales, field).
- **Mistakes:** listing every channel; "viral growth" without a loop; "partnerships" without names or stage.
- **Visual:** a funnel with real conversion numbers or a channel table.

## 9. Competition / alternatives
- **Job:** show you understand what customers do today and why you win.
- **Good:** current alternatives including status quo; two axes that matter to the buyer (not ones chosen to make you win); a one-line wedge.
- **Mistakes:** "no competitors"; 2x2 with you alone top-right on meaningless axes; dismissing incumbents.
- **Visual:** comparison table of 3–5 alternatives on 3–4 buyer criteria, or an honest 2x2.

## 10. Team
- **Job:** show why this team will win this market.
- **Good:** founders only (plus key hires), each with one line of relevant proof (built X, sold to Y, lived the problem). Founder-market fit stated explicitly.
- **Mistakes:** logo walls without context; advisor lists longer than the team; titles without proof.
- **Visual:** photo, name, role, one proof line each.

## 11. The ask & use of funds
- **Job:** say how much, what it buys, and what milestone it reaches.
- **Good:** amount, runway in months, the milestone that makes the next round possible (e.g. "$X MRR and 2 repeatable channels"), use of funds in 3–4 buckets.
- **Mistakes:** no milestone; use of funds as percentages only; valuation on the slide.
- **Visual:** milestone timeline with spend buckets below.

## 12. Vision
- **Job:** show where this goes if it works, in a believable sequence.
- **Good:** wedge → expansion → end state, each step tied to an advantage earned in the previous one.
- **Mistakes:** vision disconnected from the wedge; "the operating system for X" with no path.
- **Visual:** 3-step expansion path.

## Optional slides
- **Demo:** for presented decks; keep under 2 minutes; have a video backup.
- **Customer quote:** only real, attributable (with permission), and specific to the pain.
- **Roadmap:** only if it shows sequencing logic; avoid feature backlogs.

## Common deck-wide mistakes
- Headlines that are labels ("Market") instead of claims.
- More than three points per slide.
- Numbers without period, definition, or source.
- Inconsistent metrics between slides (MRR on one, ARR on another, without saying so).
- Design that hides weak content. Fix the content first.
- Ending on "Thank you" instead of the ask.
