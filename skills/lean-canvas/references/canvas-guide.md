# Lean Canvas block guide

Read this when filling or reviewing a canvas. Each block has: the question it answers, prompts to ask the founder, a weak and a strong example, and a quality check. Examples are illustrative only, not real companies or data.

## Why this order

The fill order front-loads the blocks that kill most early companies: nobody has the problem badly enough, or the customer you picked cannot be reached. Solution comes fourth on purpose. Founders tend to start with the solution and back-fill a customer to justify it; the order prevents that.

## 1. Customer segments + early adopters

**Question:** Who has the problem, and who has it worst right now?

Prompts:
- Who pays, and who uses? Are they the same person?
- Which subset is already trying to solve this (hacking together tools, paying someone, complaining publicly)?
- Could you name 10 of them, or find them in a single afternoon?

Weak: "Small businesses." / "Anyone who cooks."
Strong: "Independent physiotherapy clinics with 1–3 therapists in the UK. Early adopters: clinics that already moved booking online and still reconcile insurance claims by hand."

Quality check: the early-adopter description must be narrow enough that the founder can list where to find them. If they cannot, narrow it.

## 2. Problem + existing alternatives

**Question:** What are the top 1–3 problems, and what do people do about them today?

Prompts:
- What happens if they do nothing? What does it cost them in money, time, risk, or status?
- What do they use today? Include spreadsheets, email, an intern, an agency, a competitor, or ignoring it.
- What have they spent (money or hours) on alternatives? Only founder-provided figures.

Weak: "Managing finances is hard."
Strong: "Claim rejections take 3–5 hours a week to rework [ASSUMPTION, from 4 founder interviews]. Alternative: the office manager works through a spreadsheet plus the insurer's portal."

Quality check: "nothing" is a valid alternative but a warning sign. If nobody is doing anything about the problem, it may not hurt enough.

## 3. Unique value proposition + high-level concept

**Question:** Why should the early adopter pay attention, and why you instead of their current alternative?

Format that works: `<end result customer wants> + <specific time frame or proof> + <addresses an objection>`.
High-level concept: a short "X for Y" analogy that makes it instantly legible, used internally and in pitches. Only use an analogy the audience already understands.

Weak: "An AI-powered all-in-one platform that streamlines workflows."
Strong: "Rejected claims fixed and resubmitted the same day, without learning a new system."

Quality check: run the swap test. Replace the company name with a competitor's. If the sentence still works, it is not unique. See the positioning skill for depth.

## 4. Solution

**Question:** What is the smallest thing that addresses each listed problem?

Rules:
- One line per problem, numbered to match.
- Describe capability, not technology.
- If a solution element does not map to a problem, cut it or add the problem with evidence.

Weak: "Dashboard, mobile app, integrations, AI assistant, reporting."
Strong: "1 → Auto-flags rejection reason and pre-fills the correction. 2 → Weekly summary of claims at risk."

## 5. Channels

**Question:** How will the first 10 customers find you, and how does that change at scale?

Split into two lines:
- **First 10:** usually manual. Founder network, communities, direct outreach, partnerships, events.
- **At scale:** content/SEO, paid, partnerships, sales team, marketplaces, product-led loops.

Quality check: compare channel cost to price. Human-led sales rarely works below a few thousand dollars of annual contract value. Paid acquisition rarely works when LTV is under a few hundred dollars unless conversion is strong. Flag mismatches.

## 6. Revenue streams

**Question:** How does money come in?

Capture: model (subscription, usage, transaction fee, take rate, one-off, services), price or range, billing frequency, who pays. Tag the price `[ASSUMPTION]` unless customers have paid it. Hand detailed design to pricing-strategy.

## 7. Cost structure

**Question:** What does it cost to run the business and to serve one more customer?

Split:
- **Fixed monthly:** salaries (including a market-rate figure for founders if they are unpaid, labelled), tools, hosting baseline, rent.
- **Variable per customer:** hosting/inference per customer, third-party API calls, payment processing, onboarding and support time, shipping or fulfilment.

Variable cost per customer feeds gross margin and contribution margin.

## 8. Key metrics

**Question:** Which 1–3 numbers tell you whether this is working at your current stage?

By stage (pick, do not list all):
- Idea / validation: interviews completed, % confirming the top problem, number of pre-commitments (LOIs, deposits, pilots).
- MVP: activation rate (reached first value), week-4 retention, conversion to paid.
- Early traction: MRR growth rate, logo churn, CAC payback.
- Growth: net revenue retention, LTV:CAC by channel, burn multiple.

Reject vanity metrics: page views, signups without activation, social followers, waitlist size without commitment.

## 9. Unfair advantage

**Question:** What do you have that cannot be easily copied or bought?

Accept with evidence: proprietary data, owned audience or distribution, network effects already visible, regulatory licence or certification, deep insider expertise with track record, exclusive partnerships, existing customer relationships.
Reject without evidence: passion, hard work, the team, first mover, better UX, "we use AI", low price.

"None yet" is honest and fine at idea stage. It becomes a risk to note, not a box to fake.

## Versioning

- Date every canvas and number it (v1, v2, ...). Keep old versions in the journal entry so learning is visible.
- Change one block at a time after a test, and note what evidence triggered the change.
- A canvas older than 90 days at an early stage is probably stale. Suggest a refresh.

## Common failure patterns

| Pattern | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Solution-first | Solution block is the longest and most detailed | Restart from block 1 with one segment |
| Segment blur | "B2B and B2C" or 3+ segments in one canvas | One canvas per segment |
| Problem as missing feature | "There is no app for X" | Ask what it costs them today |
| Alternatives ignored | Block 2 says "no competitors" | List what they do today, even manual work |
| Vanity metrics | Followers, signups, page views | Swap for stage-appropriate metrics |
| Fake moat | Unfair advantage is a value statement | Write "none yet" and log it as a risk |
