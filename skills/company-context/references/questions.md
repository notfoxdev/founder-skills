# Question bank

Max 3 questions per message, one batch per message, every question skippable. Use the example answer in brackets only when the founder seems unsure what level of detail is wanted. Skip any question the existing file or pasted material already answers.

## Batch 1: Snapshot

1. What are you building, in one line, and for whom? (e.g. "Scheduling for physio clinics with 2–10 staff")
2. Which stage fits best: idea, validation, mvp, early-traction, or growth? (definitions below; offer them only if asked or if the answer is unclear)
3. How do you make money (or plan to, and roughly at what price), and do you have a website yet? (b2b-saas, b2c, marketplace, services, other; URL or "none yet")

Business model can often be inferred from the one-liner. Propose it as "Sounds like B2B SaaS, right?" instead of asking cold.

## Batch 2: Problem and customer

1. What problem do you solve, and who has it? (role and company type, not "businesses")
2. Who pays, who uses it day to day, and how do they buy? (card, invoice, procurement, app store)
3. What do they do today instead, and what do you understand about this that those options miss? (tools, spreadsheets, an agency, a person, doing nothing; "I don't know yet" is a fine answer for the second half)

## Batch 3: Team and traction

1. Do you have a cofounder? Name and role if so.
2. What is your role, what in your background fits this problem, and why is now the time? (a change in tools, rules, or behavior; skip if nothing changed)
3. What numbers do you have so far? Interviews, signups, pilots, users, revenue: whatever exists, each with a count. "None yet" is a fine answer.

## Batch 4: Constraints and direction

1. What constrains you? Runway in months, time (full-time or not), budget, regulation.
2. What is the one thing that kills the company if you're wrong about it?
3. What outcome are you working toward in the next 90 days, with a number?

## Batch 5: Working preferences

1. How should agents talk to you: direct, warm, analytical, or playful?
2. Anything else they should know? (formatting, spelling, things to avoid)

## Stage definitions (for mapping, not for judging)

| Stage | Use when |
|---|---|
| idea | No product in anyone's hands; still finding or confirming the problem |
| validation | Talking to customers and testing commitment (pilots, LOIs, deposits, waitlist); no product in regular use |
| mvp | A product real users use repeatedly; little or no repeatable revenue |
| early-traction | Paying customers and revenue growing month over month; looking for a repeatable channel |
| growth | Repeatable acquisition; scaling team, channels, or markets |

Common founder words: "pre-seed" and "seed" are funding stages, not company stages; ask what exists today. "Beta" is usually mvp. "Launched" with no paying customers is usually mvp. When the founder's stage and the table disagree, show the definition and the evidence in one line and ask once. If they keep their choice, record it and note the evidence in the journal entry.

## Sharpening follow-ups (one per vague answer, then accept what you get)

| Vague answer | Follow-up |
|---|---|
| "Lots of interest" | "How many, of what? Signups, calls, pilots?" |
| "SMBs" / "businesses" | "Which role at which kind of company feels this most?" |
| "No competitors" | "What do they do today when this problem hits?" |
| "Grow" / "launch" | "What number tells you in 90 days that it worked?" |
| Three risks | "Which one, if wrong, ends the company first?" |
| "A few months of runway" | "Roughly how many months? Or skip it." |
| One-liner over ~50 characters | "If you had one short line on a form, what would it say?" (offer a draft; they pick) |
| "We're just better" | "Better at what, that a customer would notice the first week?" |

## Update triggers

Ask "Does anything else change?" after these, because they often move other sections:

- Stage change → biggest risk, 90-day goal.
- Pivot → problem, customer, alternatives, traction (old numbers move to the journal).
- First revenue → stage, traction, constraints (runway).
- Cofounder joins or leaves → team, constraints.
