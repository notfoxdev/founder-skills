# AI tells

Read this in step 7 and run it against the full draft. These phrases and patterns make copy read as machine-written and interchangeable. Remove them unless the founder insists, and even then say why it costs them.

This is the short list for landing pages. The `human-voice` skill keeps the canonical, fuller pattern list and does a deeper rewrite pass; use it when this list clears but the page still sounds generic.

## Banned phrases

| Tell | Why it fails | Rewrite direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "It's not X, it's Y" / "X isn't just A, it's B" | Argues against a claim nobody made; the reflex pattern of generated copy | State Y directly with a specific |
| "Not just X, but Y" | Same pattern, softer | Say Y |
| Negation triplets: "No X. No Y. No Z." | Rhythm with no information | Name the one thing removed and what replaces it |
| "Unlock", "unleash", "harness" | Metaphor with no picture | The actual verb: see, send, approve, find |
| "Seamless", "seamlessly", "effortless", "frictionless" | Unfalsifiable | Name the step that disappeared or the time it takes |
| "In today's fast-paced world", "In an ever-changing landscape" | Throat-clearing | Delete; start with the reader's situation |
| "Leverage", "empower", "elevate", "supercharge", "turbocharge" | Hype verbs | Plain verbs |
| "Revolutionary", "game-changing", "next-generation", "cutting-edge", "state-of-the-art" | Superlatives without proof | Delete or show the evidence |
| "Reimagine", "redefine", "transform the way you" | Vague ambition | What changes, concretely |
| "Say goodbye to X", "Say hello to Y" | Template opener | Describe the new state |
| "The future of X" | Unprovable | What it does now |
| "Whether you're a X or a Y" | Avoids choosing an audience | Pick the reader |
| "All-in-one", "one-stop shop", "everything you need" | Says nothing about fit | Name the 2–3 jobs it replaces |
| "Built for the modern X" | Who is the unmodern X? | Name the audience precisely |
| "Designed to help you", "helps you" | Hedged, indirect | Make the product the subject: "Flags double bookings" |
| "Robust", "powerful", "intuitive", "scalable" | Adjectives readers skip | Show the property with a detail |
| "Dive in", "delve", "embark", "journey" | Generated register | Plain verbs, or cut |
| "Imagine a world where..." | Speculative opener | Show the real current state |
| "Magic", "magically", "like magic" | Hides the mechanism | Say what it does |
| "Here's the thing", "The result?", "The best part?" | Fake conversational reveal | Just say it |
| "At scale" with no number | Unfalsifiable | Give the scale |
| "Peace of mind" | Abstract | Name the risk removed |

## Punctuation and formatting tells

- **Em dashes in headlines and subheads.** Use a full stop, colon, or comma. In body copy, limit to one per section at most; prefer commas.
- **Exclamation marks.** None. Confidence comes from specifics.
- **Emoji** in headlines, bullets, or CTAs.
- **Title Case On Every Word** in headlines. Use sentence case unless the brand already uses title case.
- **Colons used for dramatic reveal** ("One tool: endless possibilities").
- **Rhetorical question headlines** ("Tired of spreadsheets?") unless the question is exactly what the reader types into search.
- **Ellipses** for suspense.

## Structural tells

- Every section the same length and shape.
- Everything in threes: three adjectives, three bullets, three benefits, everywhere.
- Every bullet starting with a gerund ("Streamlining...", "Enhancing...").
- Paired abstractions: "faster and smarter", "simple yet powerful", "secure and reliable".
- A closing line that summarises what the reader just read.
- Benefits written as feelings ("Feel confident", "Stay in control") with no mechanism.
- Every heading an imperative ("Discover", "Explore", "Experience").
- Hedging stacks: "can help potentially reduce".
- Mirrored sentence pairs: "Less busywork. More real work."

## Quick rewrite examples

These show the shape of a fix, with placeholders where facts are needed.

- Before: "Unlock seamless collaboration for modern teams."
  After: "Every shift swap, approved in the group chat your staff already use." (needs: confirmed integration)
- Before: "It's not just a CRM, it's your growth engine."
  After: "See which leads went quiet this week and who should call them." (needs: confirmed feature)
- Before: "No spreadsheets. No chaos. No stress."
  After: "Your stock counts update when the delivery is scanned, not when someone finds time to type them in."
- Before: "Trusted by thousands of businesses worldwide."
  After: "[NEED: customer count with source and date]" or "In private beta with 12 independent retailers."

## Final pass

1. Search the draft for every phrase in the table (case-insensitive) and for `—`, `!`, and emoji.
2. Read the hero and final CTA aloud. If you would not say it to a customer face to face, rewrite it.
3. Swap test: replace the company name with a competitor's. Every sentence that still works needs a specific added or should be cut.
