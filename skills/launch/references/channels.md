# Channel playbooks

Read this after picking channels in step 3. Platform rules change; tell the founder to re-read each platform's current guidelines in the week before launch. Anything marked "heuristic" is common practice, not a rule.

## Owned

### Email list and waitlist
- Your highest-intent channel. Send the launch email first, before rented channels, so your warmest people see it while you can still reply.
- Plain, personal, from the founder's name. One CTA. Subject line says what happened ("[Product] is live: <what it does>").
- Segment: beta users (ask for feedback, a review, or a reply), waitlist (invite with access), general list (announcement).
- Follow-up 2–3 days later to non-clickers with a different angle (demo video, a customer story), not the same email.
- Measure clicks and replies, not opens (open tracking is unreliable).

### Website and blog
- Launch post on your own domain: why you built it, who it is for, what it does, what is next. Link everything else here.
- Changelog page for feature launches; it compounds into a record of momentum.

### In-product and existing customers
- In-app banner or modal for feature launches, dismissible and shown once.
- Personal message to every existing customer before public launch. They are the most likely source of permissioned quotes.

## Rented

### Product Hunt
Fits: developer tools, AI tools, productivity, design, prosumer and consumer apps. Weaker for vertical B2B where buyers are not on PH.

Prep (start by T-14):
- Maker account active for some time with genuine participation (comment on others' launches). New accounts with no history get less reach (heuristic).
- Listing (verify current specs in the PH submission form; they change): name, tagline (60 characters max at the time of writing), description (write the key point in the first 260 characters, which is what previews tend to show), up to 3 topics, gallery images (1270x760 recommended), thumbnail (240x240, GIF allowed), optional YouTube video.
- Maker comment ready: who you are, why you built it, what it does, what is free, what feedback you want.
- Schedule the launch in advance. At the time of writing, launches go live at 12:01 AM Pacific and the day runs 24 hours on PT; verify current timing.
- Self-hunting is normal; a well-known hunter is optional.

Norms:
- Do not ask people to upvote. Tell your network you launched and ask for feedback or to check it out. PH detects and penalises vote patterns from new or linked accounts.
- Reply to every comment the same day, specifically.
- Being featured on the homepage is decided by PH curation; a launch can get reach without a top-5 finish.
- Weekdays have more traffic and more competition; weekends less of both (heuristic). Pick by whether your goal is reach or ranking.
- Offer a launch-day deal only if you would honour it for everyone who asks later.

### Hacker News (Show HN)
Fits: developer tools, infrastructure, open source, technical products, unusual technical approaches. Poor fit for marketing-led consumer apps.

Rules (from HN's Show HN guidelines; re-read before posting):
- Show HN is for something you made that people can try now. Landing pages, sign-up pages, waitlists, newsletters, fundraisers, and blog posts are not Show HN; if it is not ready to try, wait.
- The project should be non-trivial and something you are around to discuss. Minor version bumps usually do not qualify; a major overhaul may.
- Make it easy to try without barriers like signups or emails where possible. A demo, playground, or open-source repo works.
- Title starts with `Show HN:` followed by the product name and a plain description. No marketing language, no superlatives, no exclamation marks.
- Do not ask friends or colleagues to upvote or comment. HN detects voting rings and penalises them.

Approach:
- Post text or first comment from the maker: what it is, why you built it, how it works technically, what is rough, what you want feedback on. Write like an engineer talking to engineers.
- Stay online for 4–6 hours after posting and answer every substantive comment, including the harsh ones, without defensiveness.
- Timing debates are endless; weekday US morning (Pacific) is a common choice (heuristic). Pick a time you can stay at the keyboard.
- If it sinks without discussion, HN sometimes allows a repost later; check the guidelines or email the moderators rather than reposting repeatedly.

### LinkedIn
Fits: B2B almost always.
- Post from the founder's personal profile, not only the company page; personal profiles get far more reach (heuristic).
- Native video or a document carousel over a bare link. Many founders put the link in the first comment; test both.
- Structure: a first line that names the buyer's problem, the story of building it, what it does, one CTA.
- Ask 5–10 relevant people in advance to read it when it goes live and comment if they have something real to say. Not engagement pods.
- Follow with direct messages to warm contacts who match the target customer, personalised.

### X (Twitter)
Fits: dev tools, AI, consumer tech, founder-led brands with an existing following.
- Native video in the first post. A thread of 4–8 posts: problem, what it does, demo, how it works, who it is for, CTA.
- Pin the launch post. Quote-post replies and feedback through the day.
- Reach without a following is limited; pair with personal outreach.

### Reddit
Fits: consumer, prosumer, niche hobbies, some technical audiences.
- Read each subreddit's rules. Many ban self-promotion or allow it only in weekly threads.
- Participate before posting. Accounts that only post their own links get removed.
- Write for the community: what problem, what you built, what you want feedback on. Disclose you are the maker.
- If unsure, message the moderators and ask whether a launch post is welcome.

### TikTok, Reels, Shorts
Fits: consumer and creator products; some B2B with a visible problem (trades, hospitality, retail).
- A series beats a single launch video. Plan 5–10 short posts across T-14 to T+14.
- Founder-on-camera and screen-recorded demos work organically; polished ads usually do not.
- Use `video-script` for hooks and structure.

### Communities (Slack, Discord, forums, Indie Hackers)
- Only communities where the target customer actually is. One relevant 300-person Slack beats a 50,000-member general startup group.
- For vertical B2B, ask interviewees where they ask peers for help: professional associations, practitioner forums, software user communities, regional groups. Those beat any startup community.
- Follow posting rules and channel conventions (often a #launches or #show-and-tell channel).
- Tailor the post to the community: what is relevant to them, and an offer if appropriate.
- Indie Hackers suits founder-story posts with real numbers.

### Pre-launch directories (BetaList and similar)
- Useful for early waitlist signups in tech-savvy audiences; low intent for most B2B.
- Submit 2–4 weeks before public launch; some have review queues.
- Avoid paying for mass directory submissions. They rarely bring buyers.

## Borrowed

### Newsletters and podcasts
- Find 5–15 in the target customer's niche. Read recent issues; pitch a story relevant to their readers, not an announcement.
- Pitch 3–4 weeks ahead; many plan issues in advance.
- Sponsorships: worth testing only after the message converts on owned channels. Ask for audience size and past sponsor results, and start with one issue.

### Partners and integration marketplaces
- If you integrate with a platform (Slack, Shopify, HubSpot, Notion, Zapier, Chrome Web Store, accounting and practice-management app stores), list there. Most marketplaces require a working integration and a review; it can take days to weeks, so submit by T-21. No integration yet: skip the channel rather than building one for the launch. Check each marketplace's current listing requirements.
- Ask partners for a co-announcement, a newsletter mention, or a spot in their marketplace "new" section.

### Creators and influencers
- Fits consumer and prosumer. Micro-creators in the exact niche usually beat large general ones.
- Give early access, not a script. Disclosure of sponsorship is required (FTC and local equivalents).
- Agree terms in writing: deliverables, dates, usage rights.

### Press
- Early-stage launches rarely get coverage on announcement alone. A story (unusual founder, real data, a timely angle) is what gets picked up.
- Target 5–10 reporters who have covered the category in the last 6 months. Short personal pitch, embargo date if offered, assets ready.
- Trade publications in the customer's industry often matter more than tech press for B2B.

### Investors and advisors
- Send investors and advisors a short forwardable note with the link, the one-line pitch, and who you want introductions to.
