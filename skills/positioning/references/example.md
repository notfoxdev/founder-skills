## Model answer

# Positioning: Ledgerline (2026-10-21)
Status: hypothesis (0 customers, 2 pilots starting)
Bottom line: big fish, small pond as "month-end close tracking for bookkeeping firms", positioned against the Google Sheets close checklist. One-liner: "Know which clients are blocked before month-end deadlines slip. Close tracking for bookkeeping firms."

Don't position against Karbon and TaxDome. Your buyers already pay for them. If you set yourself up as their rival, buyers compare you on a practice-management feature grid you lose, and you look like a replacement they will not buy. Your problem statement says firms run close out of spreadsheets and email. That spreadsheet is what you replace. Karbon and TaxDome become an objection you answer, not the frame.

## Category reasoning
| Candidate label | Buyers expect (competitors, features, price) | Helps or hurts | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Practice management software | Karbon, TaxDome; CRM, billing, client portal; their price [NEED: verify on their sites] | Hurts: feature grid you lose, reads as a replacement | Reject |
| Close checklist | Google Sheets, free templates; a list of tasks; near free | Hurts: anchors price at zero | Reject |
| Month-end close tracking for bookkeeping firms | The spreadsheet as the comparison; status by client, deadlines, reconciliation; price [NEED: test] | Helps: frames you against the sheet and beside the practice tool | Choose |

Head-to-head fails: Karbon and TaxDome set the buying criteria for practice management, and winning that category is not your goal. New category fails two of four conditions: "close tracking" already describes you without misleading anyone, and 7 months of runway cannot fund category education. Big fish, small pond fits: close tracking is understood, and small firms closing 20–80 clients at once need a cross-client view that a per-client checklist does not give.

## Messaging
**Positioning statement (internal)**
For bookkeeping firms with 3–15 staff who run month-end close for 20–80 clients from spreadsheets and email, Ledgerline is a close tracker that shows every client's status and what is already reconciled. Unlike a Google Sheets checklist, we log each reconciliation by account, with who did it and when, so reviewers stop redoing work [NEED: proof].

**One-liner**
Know which clients are blocked before month-end deadlines slip. Close tracking for bookkeeping firms. (14 words)

**Pillars**
| Pillar | Headline | Supporting line | Proof points |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | See every blocked client today | One board shows each client's close status, so the partner stops chasing it. | [NEED: proof: on-time closes in pilot vs the firm's prior month]; mechanism: status per client |
| 2 | Review once, not twice | Each reconciliation shows who did it and when. | [NEED: proof: rework hours before and during the pilot] |
| 3 | Deadlines you can see coming | Overdue clients are flagged before the deadline, not after. | [NEED: proof: count of flagged-then-closed-on-time clients in pilot] |

**Objections**
| Objection | Response | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| "We pay for Karbon or TaxDome. Can't it do this?" | Keep it. Ledgerline tracks the close itself, client by client and account by account. Your practice tool keeps everything else. | [NEED: verify what their templates track]; [NEED: proof: a pilot firm using both] |
| "Our spreadsheet is free and works." | It works until a close slips or a reviewer redoes a reconciliation. What did the last slipped close cost you? | [NEED: proof: rework hours from pilot] |
| "Another tool for staff to update." | Ticking off a reconciliation replaces updating the sheet; it does not add a step. | [ASSUMPTION: confirm minutes per update in pilot] |
| "Won't Karbon or TaxDome just add this?" | They might. Our answer is depth on small-firm close and shipping what pilot firms ask for each week. | CEO's 4 years running ops at a 9-person firm (F); [NEED: proof: pilot change log] |
